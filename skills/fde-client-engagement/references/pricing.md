# Pricing

Price against the value created, not your hours. Early on you have no proof, so start free and climb.

## The ladder
| Step | Who | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Free first project | Anyone with no track record | [Source] Trade it for data access, a quote and permission to publish the result |
| Fixed build fee plus monthly fee | Small business | [Adapted] Monthly fee covers running cost and support |
| Five figures | After one measured result | [Source] Moves from free or low five figures to high five figures |
| Value slice | Enterprise | [Source] About 10 percent of the value delivered. $10M of value gives a $1M fee |
| Carry | Private equity | [Source] A small ownership share, for example 0.5 percent, tied to the increase in portfolio value |

The source does not cover how to move a client onto a monthly retainer. The monthly fee below is an adaptation.

## Price from the baseline [Adapted]
Recovered revenue per month = quotes sent x share never followed up x share that reply after a follow-up x close rate x average job value.

Use the client's measured numbers. `scripts/recovered_revenue.py` does the arithmetic and shows a fee at a chosen share of the recovered revenue.

Sanity checks:
- The fee should be a small fraction of what it recovers, so the decision is easy.
- The monthly fee should cover running cost with margin. Keep a written list of running costs: hosting, model usage, messaging, tools.
- If the baseline shows little recovered value, say so and shrink the scope.

## Calibrate to the market [Adapted]
Enterprise anchors do not transfer to a small shop in a low-income market. Check what the local alternatives cost (a part-time assistant, a local bot builder, a subscription tool) and price below the cost of doing nothing. Quote in the client's currency.

## Be careful with
- Promising benchmark results.
- Charging for hours when the client is buying an outcome.
- Quoting before you have a baseline, except for the free first project.
